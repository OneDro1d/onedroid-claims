## 1. Who does what

The question "can I trust this content" is answered in five steps, two of them judgement and three mechanical (Michal, 11 Nov 2026): list the claims; look for evidence for and against each one; decide what the evidence shows; seal content and evidence together; score.

- **The auditor** (steps 1 and 2) lists the claims and gathers the evidence for and against each: saved copies of the sources, verbatim quotes, what it searched and did not find. The auditor publishes no verdicts. Its part is `audit.jsonl` with `sources/`, signed with its key (section 3a). This is the costly step (a strong model over saved copies, two passes). In the claims session's runs of 7 to 9 Nov 2026 those two passes graded and cited in the same row (the prove pass found the artefact and called the claim evidenced in one breath), so a 0.2 or 0.3 snapshot converted to 0.4 is split, not re-audited: the refs become the auditor's candidates, the grade and its note become the witness's decision, and the witness header says so (sections 4b and 10).
- **The witness** (step 3) reads each claim against the evidence the auditor gathered and decides: evidenced, contradicted, no evidence published, on request, with a confidence and a note. A witness may also say that the page makes a claim the auditor did not list. Its part is one file under `witness/`, signed with its key over the auditor part's hash. There can be more than one witness: anyone who re-does the witness work on a published snapshot publishes one more file, and that is the cheap way to validate a snapshot for oneself, by an agent or by a person reading claim by claim. What a second witness validates is the decision over the evidence the auditor GATHERED, not the completeness of the search: a grader that stopped looking and settled on NO EVIDENCE PUBLISHED agrees with any witness who reads the same candidates. Missed evidence is caught by the holdout (graded from the sources, next bullet) and by a witness's `evidence_missing`, never by the agreement figure.
- **The author** of the document is normally the auditor and the first witness (a self-audit, said so in the manifest, with a withheld holdout: a sample of claims the orchestrator grades from the SAVED SOURCES before any pass row is opened and independently of `audit.jsonl`, so it catches evidence the auditor missed as well as verdicts the witness got wrong). The two roles stay distinct in the files even when one session plays both, because that is what lets a second witness come later without redoing the auditor's work.
- **The scripts** (steps 4 and 5) do the rest with no judgement: `merge.py` (the auditor's rows with the primary witness's verdicts = `claims.jsonl`), the score (evidence-score/1), `check.py` (section 5), and the seal (the manifest with every file's sha256, the signatures, the anchors).
- **A reader, or a cheap model**, may run the optional reading pass: does each quote actually support its claim? That pass never changes a verdict; it records disagreements for the author.
- **A catalogue** (later) fetches snapshots, runs the checker, and scores and lists the documents, sites, posts and profiles that publish them.

## 2. Layout

One folder per subject per snapshot date, immutable once published. A change to the document means a new snapshot.

```
<publisher>/<subject-slug>/<YYYY-MM-DD>/
  manifest.json        what, when, by whom, counts and score, every file with its sha256
  claims.jsonl         one claim per line (section 4)
  page.txt             the audited text, one block per line (a site: one file per audited surface under pages/, as served)
  sources/NNN.txt      text copies of the cited sources as graded, when the licence allows (section 6)
  sources/repo/<path>  a site audit: the files of the publisher's public repository that the grader cited, at the pinned commit
  CHECK.txt            the checker's output when the author ran it (optional, informative)
  audit.jsonl          0.4: the auditor's part, claims and candidate evidence, no verdicts (section 4a)
  witness/<name>.jsonl 0.4: one file per witness, a decision per claim id (section 4b)
  signatures.json      0.4: who signed which part, over which hash, when (section 3a)
  anchors.json         0.4, optional: where each signature's hash was anchored in time (section 3a)
  CHECK.json           0.4: the checker's run as data, the verification block another system can carry (section 5)
```

A 0.3 snapshot has `claims.jsonl` alone. A 0.4 snapshot has `audit.jsonl` and at least one witness file, and its `claims.jsonl` is their merge, so every 0.3 reader still works.

## 3. manifest.json

```json
{
  "format": "claims-snapshot/0.2",
  "subject": {"title": "...", "url": "https://...", "publisher": "...", "parts": ["https://...", "..."]},
  "taken_at": "2026-11-09T15:00:00Z",
  "pages": [{"file": "page.txt", "sha256": "...", "lines": 222, "fetched_at": "...", "note": "..."}],
  "sources": [
    {"n": 1, "url": "https://...", "file": "sources/001.txt", "sha256": "...", "copy": "text",
     "fetched_at": "...", "status": 200, "body_sha256": "...", "text_copy_of": "html"},
    {"n": 24, "url": "https://.../card.pdf", "file": null, "sha256": "...", "copy": "hash-only", "text_copy_of": "pdf-text",
     "extractor": {"note": "...", "files": [{"file": "work/pdftext2.py", "sha256": "..."}]}},
    {"n": 31, "url": "https://...", "file": null, "sha256": null, "copy": "none", "status": 403,
     "note": "answered 403 to a scripted GET; quotes via a summarising fetch, not checkable"}
  ],
  "grading": {"kind": "self-audit | split", "profile": "cited-sources", "grader": "...", "model": "...", "method": "...",
              "graded_at": "...",
              "auditor":   {"name": "...", "model": "...", "signer": "name <key id>", "file": "audit.jsonl", "sha256": "..."},
              "witnesses": [{"name": "...", "model": "... | human", "method": "...", "signer": "name <key id>",
                             "file": "witness/<name>.jsonl", "sha256": "...", "primary": true,
                             "score": {"coverage_pct": 96, "contradiction_pct": 0, "band": "A"}, "missing_claims": 0}],
              "sessions":  [{"role": "auditor | witness", "recorder": "onedroid-indie 0.1.0-dev (1df3805)", "session_id": "...",
                             "machine_id": "m-...", "record_sha256": "...", "complete": true, "gaps": 0,
                             "held_by": "...", "available": "on request"}],
              "gates": [{"run": "first grade", "size": 21, "sealed_sha256": "...", "sealed_at": "...",
                         "first_gate": "failed 20/21 (C-025)", "regraded_ids": ["C-025"], "holdout_amended": false,
                         "amended_sha256": null, "final": "passed 21/21 after the re-grade"}],
              "rounds": [{"date": "...", "what": "..."}]},
  "counts": {"claims": 136, "checkable": 136, "evidenced": 130, "on_request": 0, "no_evidence": 6, "contradicted": 0,
             "contradicted_high": 0, "withdrawn": 0, "author_read": 6, "removed_since_grading": 2},
  "score": {"coverage_pct": 96, "contradiction_pct": 0, "band": "A", "formula": "evidence-score/1"},
  "claims_sha256": "..."
}
```

- `taken_at` is when the page text was taken. Sources carry their own `fetched_at`. `pages` has one entry per audited file; a single document has one. A site's page entries carry the `url` each surface was served from (so `--fetch` can look for every claim on the live page) and the manifest a `pages_note` saying how they were taken.
- `copy` is `text` (a text copy is in `sources/`), `hash-only` (the author holds the copy; its hash is published; quotes are checkable by a live fetch or by the author) or `none` (nothing saved: the row's quotes from it are not checkable, and the checker says so). A text copy made from a PDF or from a fetched HTML body records the extractor scripts and their hashes, because the hash is of the extraction, not the body. A hash-only source may name the author's private file as `copy_name` (a site run's `fetched/NNN.txt`, numbered by fetch order) so `--copies` finds it. A source taken from a public repository carries `repo` (`github`, `commit`, `path`, `licence`) and its `url` is the raw file at that commit. A source the grader's worker fetched live and did not save is `copy: none` with the `status` it answered; the status is its whole record.
- `grading.profile` names which evidence rule applied (section 4). `grading.gates` records every holdout gate: the sealed holdout's hash and time, the first gate's result as it happened, the ids re-graded afterwards, whether the holdout itself was amended (and the amended file's hash), and the final result. "Passed" alone is not a record. The holdout is graded from the saved sources, not from `audit.jsonl`: a gate that only re-reads the auditor's candidates cannot see a missed source.
- `counts` and `score` are recomputed by the checker from `claims.jsonl` with the published formula (section 5); a manifest that disagrees fails.
- 0.4: `grading.kind` is `self-audit` when the auditor and the primary witness are the same signer, `split` otherwise. `auditor` and `witnesses` name each part, its signer and its hash; exactly one witness is `primary`, and `claims.jsonl` carries that witness's decisions. Each witness entry carries its own `score`, recomputed by the checker, and `missing_claims`, the number of MISSING CLAIM rows it filed. `sessions` lists the recorded agent sessions that produced each part: the recorder and its version, the session and machine ids, the sha256 of the session record, whether the recorder says the record is complete and how many gaps it has, who holds it and how to get it. The record itself is not in the folder: a transcript carries whatever was in the window, including things that must never be public (a planted credential was found in Claude Code's own transcript on 10 Nov 2026), so the hash is published and the record is shown on request.

## 3a. Signatures and anchors (0.4)

- **What is signed.** One line of text per part, so that a stranger can rebuild it from the published bytes: `claims-snapshot/0.4 auditor audit.jsonl sha256:<hex>` for the auditor; `claims-snapshot/0.4 witness witness/<name>.jsonl sha256:<hex> over audit.jsonl sha256:<hex>` for a witness, which binds the decisions to the evidence they were made on. `signatures.json` lists `{role, file, sha256, line, signer: {name, key_id, kind}, signed_at, signature: "<file>.sig", commit: "<sha> | null"}`.
- **How.** SSH signatures: `sign.py <snapshot> --role auditor|witness --file <part> --key <private key> --identity <id>` runs `ssh-keygen -Y sign -n claims-snapshot` over the line, writes `<part>.sig`, the entry in `signatures.json` and the public key into the snapshot's `allowed_signers`; the checker verifies with `ssh-keygen -Y verify -n claims-snapshot -f allowed_signers -I <id>`. The identity is the principal of `allowed_signers`: an email address or a hyphenated name, no spaces. The keys are the ones GitHub already shows for a person at `github.com/<user>.keys`, so a signature is traced to a person without a new register. A git-signed commit of the part by the same key is the same proof in a second place, and GitHub shows it as Verified; `commit` records it when there is one.
- **An agent's key.** An agent signs with a key its human holds or has enrolled on that machine (OneDroid Indie's machine id and pass-list key are such an enrolment); the human's name is on the signature, and `sessions` says which recorded session produced the part. That is what puts a person's reputation behind an agent's work.
- **Anchors.** `anchors.json` lists `{over: "sha256:<hex of the signature file>", method: "ots | argus-commitment | armarium", ref, time, proof_file}`. An anchor proves the signed bytes existed by that time and in that order; the signature proves who. The October 2026 run used Argus commitments (anchors on Base Sepolia, argus-dev-poa and OpenTimestamps); armarium is the long-run home.
- **What none of it proves.** A signature says who, an anchor says by when, the checker says the record is intact and consistent, CHECK.json says the checker ran. None of the four says a verdict is true: that is the witness's, and the reason the auditor's part is published is that any reader can be the next witness.

## 4. claims.jsonl (the merged view)

One JSON object per line, the auditor's row with the primary witness's decision (sections 4a and 4b); in a 0.3 snapshot, the grader's row:

```json
{"id": "C-001",
 "text": "the claim as it stands on the page [...] elisions marked like this",
 "location": [{"file": "page.txt", "line": 3}],
 "kind": "figure | arithmetic | quote | citation | negative | comparison | process | rule | promise | page-internal | other",
 "checkable": true,
 "verdict": "EVIDENCED | NO EVIDENCE PUBLISHED | CONTRADICTED | EVIDENCE AVAILABLE ON REQUEST | WITHDRAWN",
 "confidence": "high | low",
 "proofs":    [{"file": "sources/010.txt", "url": "https://...", "quote": "verbatim words from the source", "verified": true}],
 "counter":   [{"file": "sources/009.txt", "url": "https://...", "quote": "the words that contradict", "verified": true}],
 "located":   [{"file": "page.txt", "quote": "the sentence on the page the grader read"}],
 "consulted": [{"file": "sources/015.txt", "url": "https://...", "quote": "what the grader found where it looked"}],
 "note": "the grader's note, plain English, no file paths",
 "history": "Re-graded 8 Nov 2026 on this wording | Corrected 8 Nov 2026, read by the author's session | null",
 "graded_by": "grader | author",
 "graded_at": "2026-11-07T23:10:00Z",
 "carried_from": null}
```

Rules:
- `text` is verbatim from the audited page, markers like `[3]` and end punctuation aside; `[...]` marks an elision; every fragment must be on the page. `location` names the file and line of each fragment.
- **Verdict rule, cited-sources profile** (a brief, a paper, an article): CONTRADICTED if a source the page cites, or the page itself, says the opposite; else EVIDENCED if the saved copy of the cited source carries the same figure, date, entity, measure or words; else NO EVIDENCE PUBLISHED.
- **Verdict rule, publisher-surfaces profile** (a website, a repository, a profile): EVIDENCED if an independent, checkable artefact exists on the publisher's own surfaces, or in a public register named in `sources`, that would be false if the claim were false; a claim that is merely restated on another surface is NO EVIDENCE PUBLISHED; CONTRADICTED when two of the publisher's surfaces disagree, or a public register disagrees with a registration claim. Every URL a grader cites is saved and listed in `sources`; an artefact that was fetched and not saved is `copy: none` and does not count for the checker.
- EVIDENCE AVAILABLE ON REQUEST: the publisher holds the evidence and says so; counted in coverage, marked. WITHDRAWN: the publisher withdrew the claim; not counted. A claim of kind `promise` (a statement about the future) is not checkable and is left out of every denominator.
- An EVIDENCED claim has at least one proof. A proof is a verbatim quote from the named file. Two other shapes exist for site audits: an **existence proof** (`file` named, `quote: null`), where the artefact is the file itself at the pinned commit or the saved copy of a page that answered (verified when the copy is at hand and hash-checked; never valid for one of the publisher's own audited pages, whose existence proves nothing); and a **status proof** (`url` and `expect_status`, no file), where the artefact is that a URL answered with that status (not checkable offline, compared live by `--fetch`; it may carry the `quote` the grader saw there, which `--fetch` looks for). A long cited range is quoted by its first non-empty line, said so in the proof's `note`; the file's hash covers the rest.
- **Cited-sources profile:** a quote from the audited page proves only a claim about the page itself (`kind: page-internal`) or the page's own arithmetic from inputs it states (`kind: arithmetic`); those kinds are assigned by the lister or by hand and listed in the converter's configuration, never inferred from where the proofs happen to point. For any other kind a page quote is a locator and goes in `located`. **Publisher-surfaces profile:** the audited pages are the publisher's surfaces, and whether a surface is the artefact (a terms page for a liability cap, a changelog for a dated change) or a mere restatement is the grader's judgement, not the checker's; a page quote therefore counts as a proof, and the checker warns for every row verified by page quotes alone.
- A row may also carry `searched` (what the grader looked for and did not find, as text), `other_pass_note` (the other pass's note, when two passes graded), `on_request` (`holder`, `sha256` of the private bundle, `shown`, `seen_by`, `what`: required for EVIDENCE AVAILABLE ON REQUEST), `carried_from` (the earlier run a row was carried from unchanged) and `lister` (the lister's own kind, checkability grade and status, kept for the record).
- A CONTRADICTED claim has at least one counter quote and a `confidence`. `low` is set when the grader's note says so (the words "low" and "confiden" both appear); everything else is `high`.
- A NO EVIDENCE PUBLISHED claim carries no proofs. `located` says where on the page it is; `consulted` records what the grader found where it looked.
- `graded_by` and `graded_at` name who decided the row and when. A row read by the author after the grade (a correction) says `author`; the counts report `author_read`, and the checker says so. Rows carried unchanged from an earlier snapshot say `carried_from`.
- `proofs[].verified` is set by the checker, never by hand.
- Kinds: `figure` (a number, price or date), `arithmetic` (the page's own calculation), `quote` (words attributed to a source), `citation` (a cited URL resolves and is what the page says it is), `negative` ("none", "no", "never"), `comparison`, `process` (how something is done), `rule` (a law, standard or policy says), `promise` (not checkable), `page-internal` (a statement about the page itself), `other`.

## 4a. audit.jsonl, the auditor's part (0.4)

One row per claim, the `claims.jsonl` row without its decision: `id`, `text`, `location`, `kind`, `checkable`, `proofs` (the candidate evidence FOR, as found, each a verbatim quote from a named saved copy), `counter` (the candidate evidence AGAINST, the same shape), `located`, `consulted`, `searched`, `audited_by`, `audited_at`. No `verdict`, `confidence`, grader's `note`, `graded_by` or `graded_at`. A row may carry both proofs and counter candidates: the auditor records what it found, not what it concluded. The rules of section 4 for `text`, `location`, quotes, existence and status proofs, kinds and copies apply unchanged.

## 4b. witness/<name>.jsonl, a witness's part (0.4)

The first line is a header: `{"witness": "<name>", "model": "... | human", "method": "...", "over": {"file": "audit.jsonl", "sha256": "..."}, "started_at": "...", "finished_at": "..."}`. Then one row per claim: `{"id": "C-001", "verdict": "...", "confidence": "high | low", "on": {"proofs": [0, 2], "counter": []}, "note": "...", "graded_at": "..."}`, where `on` names the indices of the auditor's candidates the decision rests on (an EVIDENCED decision names at least one proof, a CONTRADICTED one at least one counter), and the verdict vocabulary is section 4's. Where the auditor's row carries one `evidence` list instead of `proofs` and `counter` (the OneDroid Indie shape, section 12), both `on.proofs` and `on.counter` index that one list, and a CONTRADICTED decision names in `on.counter` the pointer that shows the opposite (added 12 Nov 2026 on the W2 gate of the kit's witness step; the checker reads it from the same date). A witness does not add evidence: if it knows of evidence the auditor did not gather, it says so in `evidence_missing` (text) and the next snapshot's auditor fetches it. A witness may add rows the auditor has no claim for: `{"id": "M-001", "verdict": "MISSING CLAIM", "text": "<verbatim from the page>", "location": [...], "note": "..."}`; such rows are not merged into `claims.jsonl` and not scored, they are counted in the witness entry's `missing_claims` and shown by the checker, because the score covers the claims the auditor listed and this is how a witness says the list is short.

`merge.py <snapshot> --write` copies the auditor's row and adds the primary witness's `verdict`, `confidence`, `note`, `history`, `graded_by` (the role, `grader` or `author`, as in 0.3), `graded_at`, and `witness` (the witness's name); the checker rebuilds it in memory and compares row by row as JSON objects (K12). A second witness changes nothing in `claims.jsonl`; the checker computes, per extra witness, its own score and its agreement with the primary (the share of the claims both decided that carry the same verdict). `split.py SRC DST --auditor NAME --witness NAME` turns a published 0.2 or 0.3 snapshot into a new 0.4 folder (the source is never edited): the grader's rows become the auditor's part and one witness file whose decisions rest on every candidate the grader recorded, because the older format did not say which one each decision rested on. The split changes the shape, not the method: in those runs the prove and disprove passes graded and cited in one row, so the auditor's part of a converted snapshot was never a verdict-free gathering, the two parts come from the same pass, and the witness header's `note` says it was converted.

## 5. The checks and the score

`check.py <snapshot-dir> [--copies DIR] [--fetch] [--strict] [--json]` prints one line per check, PASS, WARN or FAIL, and exits 0 when nothing failed. With `--json` it also writes `CHECK.json`.

| id | check | fails when |
|---|---|---|
| K1 | manifest parses, `format` and `grading.profile` known, required fields present, every gate complete | any missing |
| K2 | every page, source copy and `claims.jsonl` has the sha256 the manifest states | a hash differs or a file is missing |
| K3 | claim ids unique; verdict, kind, `graded_by` in the vocabulary; `graded_at` present; every CONTRADICTED row has a confidence; every ON REQUEST row names the holder and the bundle hash; every WITHDRAWN row says so | any violation |
| K4 | every claim text (except WITHDRAWN) is on the audited page(s), fragments, markers and end punctuation aside | a fragment is missing |
| K5 | every EVIDENCED claim has at least one proof whose quote is verbatim in its named source copy (`sources/` or `--copies`), or an existence proof whose file is at hand; in the cited-sources profile page quotes count only for `page-internal` and `arithmetic`; in the publisher-surfaces profile they count, with a warning per row that rests on them alone | a row has no checkable proof |
| K6 | every CONTRADICTED claim has at least one counter quote verbatim in its named copy | none checkable |
| K7 | NO EVIDENCE PUBLISHED, ON REQUEST and WITHDRAWN rows carry no verified source proof | a verified proof is present |
| K8 | counts and score recomputed from `claims.jsonl` by evidence-score/1 equal the manifest, band included | any difference |
| K9 | no credential-shaped strings in any published file | a match |
| K10 | `--fetch` only: every source a proof uses, fetched now with a browser user-agent, still carries each quote; status proofs answer the recorded status; every audited page with a url still carries each claim located on it; PDFs are reported as unchecked | a quote, status or claim is gone (WARN; FAIL with `--strict`) |
| K11 | 0.4: `audit.jsonl` rows carry no decision fields; every witness row's id is in `audit.jsonl` or is a MISSING CLAIM row with text on the page and a location; every `on` index exists; an EVIDENCED decision names a proof and a CONTRADICTED one a counter; each witness header names `audit.jsonl` with its current sha256 | any violation |
| K12 | 0.4: `claims.jsonl` equals the merge of `audit.jsonl` and the primary witness file; each witness's score and `missing_claims` in the manifest equal the recomputation | any difference |
| K13 | 0.4: every entry in `signatures.json` names a file in the manifest whose sha256 matches, its line is the prescribed one, and the signature verifies with `ssh-keygen -Y verify` against `allowed_signers` when that tool is on PATH (else WARN: present, not verified here) | a binding is broken, or a verification fails |
| K14 | 0.4: every anchor's `over` is the sha256 of a file in the folder; with `--fetch`, an OpenTimestamps proof is verified when `ots` is on PATH, else reported | a binding is broken |
| K15 | 0.4: every `sessions` entry is complete (role, recorder, session id, record hash, complete flag, gap count, holder); a record file that is in the folder has the stated hash | missing or mismatched |

**CHECK.json** is the run as data: `{"engine": "check.py", "checker_sha256": "...", "format_read": "claims-snapshot/0.4", "ran_at": "...", "manifest_sha256": "...", "flags": ["--fetch"], "checks": [{"id": "K1", "status": "PASS", "text": "...", "details": [...]}], "result": "PASS | WARN | FAIL"}`. It is the verification block another record can carry by reference (an attestation record's verification layer, an Argus scenario that asserts `check.py --fetch --strict` exits 0 at a time): what ran, over which bytes, when, with what result. It certifies that the verification ran, nothing more.

**evidence-score/1** (the claims session's `evidence_score.py`, unchanged): over the checkable rows (not WITHDRAWN, not `promise`), coverage = evidenced plus on request, contradiction = contradicted, both as percentages rounded half to even; band A at 90 or more, B at 75, C at 50, D at 25, else E; any high-confidence contradiction caps the band at C; contradiction above 5% caps it at D. The band is computed, never copied from an earlier run.

WARN is for things a reader should know that are not errors: sources with `copy: none`, proofs the checker could not test for lack of a copy, rows that rest only on the page itself, individual quotes not found in their source while the row still holds on another proof, rows graded by the author, gates whose first result failed or whose holdout was amended, a live body that changed, a PDF the checker cannot read live.

The checker was tested on mutated snapshots on 9 Nov 2026 (a changed byte in the page, an altered claim, an altered quote, a flipped verdict, wrong counts, a planted token, a duplicate id, a verdict without its counter quote): each one failed the check the table names. What no check can do is judge whether a quote supports its claim; that is the grader's work or the reading pass's.

## 6. Copies of sources

A snapshot is only checkable offline if the source copies travel with it. Third-party pages are copyrighted; short quotes are the proof, full copies are a convenience. Default for a public repository: `copy: hash-only` for third-party sources (the author keeps the text copy; `--fetch` checks the quotes live) and `copy: text` for the author's own pages. A publisher who has the right to redistribute a source may set `copy: text`. The checker treats both the same when a copy is at hand.

## 7. The optional reading pass

A fixed prompt per claim, for a small model: "Here is a claim and a quote from the source it cites. Answer yes if the quote states the claim's figure, date, entity, measure or words; otherwise no, with one sentence." Output `review.jsonl` with `{id, agree, why}`. It is advisory: it never changes a verdict, and a snapshot with disagreements is still valid. Its cost sets the price of a catalogue check; the grading's cost does not.

## 8. Publishing

- One repository per publisher, or one shared repository with one folder per publisher. The folder path encodes publisher, subject and date; nothing inside is edited after publication; a correction is a new dated snapshot and the old one stays.
- The published document links to its snapshot, and the snapshot names the document's URL and the text it graded, so the two check each other.
- The grader is named. "Self-audit" is the normal case and is said so. A snapshot graded by someone other than the author says who. A snapshot whose rows were graded at different times says so row by row; a publisher who wants one date re-grades every row before publishing.

## 9. What a snapshot is not

It is not a certificate and not an independent audit. It is the author showing its work in a form a machine can check: these are my claims, here is where each one is backed, here is where I was contradicted and what I did about it, who decided each row and when, and here is the hash of everything so you can see nothing moved. The score is a reading aid for the author's own document, not a ranking against anyone else's until a catalogue runs the same checker over many snapshots.

With 0.4 the same sentence holds layer by layer: the signature says who, the anchor says by when, the checker says the folder is intact and consistent, CHECK.json says the checker ran, the session record (by its hash) says how the part was made and that nothing was hidden from the human in the loop. None of them says a verdict is true. The truth of a verdict is the witness's, so a snapshot's worth to a stranger is exactly the worth of its witnesses, which is why more than one is allowed and why the auditor's part is published for the next one. The score covers the claims the auditor listed and nothing else; a MISSING CLAIM row is the witness's way of saying the list is short, and a snapshot with such rows says so in its counts.

## 10. Converting existing runs

`build_snapshot.py` converts a cited-sources run (a brief: one page, numbered sources, `fetched/NNN`) with its delta re-grade and corrections.

`build_site_snapshot.py` converts a publisher-surfaces run (a website: onedroid.ai, optima.financial) from its saved files alone, fetching nothing. Both scripts import the checker's normalisation and score, so a converter and the checker cannot drift. What the site converter does with each thing such a run has:

- **Many audited files** (`sources/<file>`): each becomes `pages/<file>` as served, with the url the lister recorded for it (or the run's `fetch-log.json` gives); a claim's `location` lists every file and line the lister found it on. An HTML surface is matched as its rendered text (tags gone, entities decoded), because that is how the lister quotes it; the page entry says `read_as: html-text`, and the checker reads such a page the same way (K4, K5, K10). A single-line JSON or minified JavaScript surface is quoted by the fragments the grader named, else by the first 400 characters of the cited line, said in the proof's note.
- **Refs without quotes** (`sources/<file>#L<n>` or `#L<a>-<b>`, `repo/<path>#L<n>`): the cited line or lines become the quote; a range longer than six lines is quoted by its first non-empty line and the proof's note says so. A ref that names a file with no line is an existence proof for a repository file and a locator (in `consulted`) for one of the publisher's own pages. A ref whose text names what the grader saw (`fetched/005.body#200 block_number 47817413 …`) is reduced to its verbatim fragments: quoted strings first, else hashes, timestamps and long numbers; each becomes a proof, verified against the run's copy, and the whole text stays in the proof's note.
- **The repository clone at a pinned commit** (`repo/`): every cited file is copied to `sources/repo/<path>` and listed with the raw URL at that commit, the licence and the commit, so a reader can fetch the same bytes.
- **`fetched/`, numbered by fetch order:** third-party pages are hash-only by default (hash of the visible-text copy, body hash, extractor recorded), with `copy_name` so the author's `--copies` folder resolves; a fetch from one of the publisher's own hosts is published as text.
- **Worker fetches that were not saved** (`https://…#401`): a `copy: none` source with the status the worker saw, and a status proof on the row; such a row is untestable offline and the checker says so. A page quoted through a summarising fetch (no body saved) is `copy: none` and its quotes are marked as not checkable.
- **The verdict rule** is the run's own: CONTRADICTED if the disprove pass said yes, else EVIDENCED if the prove pass found an artefact, else NO EVIDENCE PUBLISHED; the converter asserts its counts against the run's `score.json`. `private-evidence.json` turns a NO EVIDENCE row into EVIDENCE AVAILABLE ON REQUEST with the bundle's hash; `judge.json` (an orchestrator's ruling) and `withdrawn.json` override a row when present, with the reason in `history`; a row whose pass note begins `carried from <run>` keeps that run's date in `graded_at` and names it in `carried_from`.
- **Confidence** of a contradiction is `low` when the disprove note says so (the words "low" and "confiden" both appear), as in evidence-score/1.

**Converting to 0.4** (`split.py`, section 4b) is a third step after either converter: the prove and disprove passes of these runs graded and cited in the same row, so the split puts each row's refs in `audit.jsonl` and its grade with its note in the witness file, with `on` naming every candidate. A converted snapshot's auditor and witness are one pass in two files; its header says so, and a later independent witness is the first real second opinion.

What converting cannot do: it never changes a verdict, never adds a quote the grader did not point at, and cannot save a page the grader's worker read live and did not save. The first site snapshot, onedroid.ai from the seventh run of 7 Nov 2026, shows the cost of that last point: 39 of 304 evidenced rows rest on unsaved live reads of the publisher's own hosts and are checkable only with `--fetch`. The next run should fetch those URLs once, like the third-party pages, so the snapshot carries their hashes.

## 12. The same shape in OneDroid Indie: the agent-work profile (0.5, built)

The kit's Stop hook asks the agent for a claims block in its final response: each claim it makes about its own work ("the tests pass", "no credential is in the record", "the endpoint answers 200") with evidence pointers. That block is the auditor's part, written by the agent that did the work. The Indie session record (every prompt, tool call and stop, sealed by the recorder) is the session evidence, by its hash. The developer, or a second agent with its own key, writes the witness file: claim by claim, does the evidence show it (`onedroid witness`). `onedroid claims bundle` writes one turn end as one snapshot folder, format `claims-snapshot/0.5`, profile `agent-work`. The verdict vocabulary, the merge, the score, the signatures and K1 to K15 are the ones above; this section says only what differs.

**The auditor's row.** `{"id", "text", "kind", "checkable"?, "note"?, "evidence": [...]}`: one `evidence` list of pointers instead of `proofs` and `counter`, each pointer one of four shapes: `{"file", "line"}`, `{"test", "output_sha256"}`, `{"command", "output_sha256"}`, `{"record": "<session>#<sequence>"}`. A witness's `on.proofs` and `on.counter` both index that list (section 4b). The kit's ask offers the agent the first three shapes only: an agent cannot know its own record's session id or sequence, so a record pointer from it is a guess (the first live run cited the parent session), and a guessed pointer is worse than none. The record shape stays in the format for callers that know the sequence: a runner, the plane, the Fleet run record (12 Nov 2026). `audit.jsonl` is the block exactly as the agent wrote it, so its sha256 is the one the kit recorded at the turn end and the one every witness header names.

**The page.** The kit keeps the block, not the whole reply, so `page.txt` is the claims' text as the agent wrote it, one per line, and K4 holds by construction. A witness's MISSING CLAIM row therefore cannot be found on a page: in this profile it needs the claim's text and a note saying where the agent made it, and no location (K11).

**The verdict rule, agent-work profile.** EVIDENCED if a pointer, followed, shows the claim (the line says it, the output is the test's pass, the record row is the call); CONTRADICTED if a pointer shows the opposite (named in `on.counter`); else NO EVIDENCE PUBLISHED. This is the witness's judgement; the checker checks only that the pointers resolve.

**Sources and the record.** A file a pointer names is copied to `sources/files/<path>` from the developer's working tree when the bundle is made, listed in `sources` with `path` (the pointer's path), `role: "file"`, its sha256 and `repo.commit` (HEAD at that time); a file that holds a credential, is larger than 1 MiB or is not found is listed `hash-only` or `none`, never copied. A path that leaves the folder, by `..`, by an absolute path or by a symbolic link in any of its components, is never read or copied: the bundle lists it as `none` and says why, because a bundle is made to be handed on. An output the developer hands in (`--output FILE`) is published as `sources/outputs/<sha256>.txt`, `role: "output"`, only when a pointer names its sha256. The session entry's `record_sha256` is the hash of the record's stored lines up to and including the turn end's entry (`record_upto_sequence`), so the hash holds while the session goes on; the record itself is in `sessions/<id>.jsonl` only when the developer passes `--with-record`.

**Signatures.** The auditor's part is signed by the machine's enrolled key (the kit's pass-list key, ed25519), over the section 3a line, as an SSH signature the kit writes itself; the identity is the human who stands behind the agent. **The witness is never the auditor** (Indie VR-I11): a witness file signed with the auditor's key, under any principal, fails K13, and the bundle refuses to write it. The primary witness must be signed unless the developer passes `--allow-unsigned`; an unsigned witness is listed with `"signer": null, "status": "unsigned: a claim, not a decision"` (VR-I13). `anchors.json` is `[]` until anchoring exists.

**What the checker does differently for this shape** (a row with `evidence` and no `proofs`/`counter`):
- K5 and K6: an EVIDENCED or CONTRADICTED row passes when at least one of its pointers resolves from the folder alone: a file pointer when `sources` lists that `path` with a copy at hand that has that line; a test or command pointer when a source with that sha256 is in the folder; a record pointer when the published session record holds that session and sequence. A pointer whose target is not in the folder (an output not handed in, the record held by hash, a file held by hash) is untestable: WARN, as a hash-only source is. A file copy without the line, or a published record without the row, FAILs that pointer; the row fails when none holds.
- K7 does not apply: a pointer that resolves shows where the agent looked, not that the claim holds, so a NO EVIDENCE PUBLISHED decision over resolving pointers is normal.
- K11: MISSING CLAIM as above. A witness file that is not JSON lines fails K11 by name (in any profile), where 0.4 stopped with a traceback.
- K13: the auditor-key rule above.

What resolves is never what is true: a file line that exists may not say what the claim says. That reading is the witness's, as everywhere in this format.

Tested 12 Nov 2026 (OneDroid Indie `internal/cli/bundlecmd_test.go`): S-I10's bundle (three claims, one false; one evidenced, one no evidence, one contradicted) checks K1 to K15 PASS with 0 warnings; under 0.4's checker the same folder, relabelled 0.4/cited-sources, passes everything but K5 and K6. Six mutants each fail a named check: one byte of `audit.jsonl` (K11, K13), one witness verdict (K11, K12, K13), a witness file that is not JSON (K11), a verdict flipped in `claims.jsonl` (K2, K12), one evidence pointer (K11, K13), the witness re-signed with the auditor's key (K13).

## 13. A test run as a claim record: the test-run profile (0.5, built; Talos's draft of 12 Nov 2026, adopted the same day with three checker rules added at the gate of Indie #59)

Every scenario Fleet runs is recorded as a claim (Michal, 11 Nov 15:0xZ, Slack C0C2V4LADEK message 1794416801.713079): what we ran, what we got, what it tells you, with the evidence, witnessed and signed. It is the third subject of the one record contract (D75 A, working assumption) beside a page (sections 1 to 11) and an agent's work (section 12): the same folder, merge, score, signatures and checks, with this profile, `test-run`. One folder per scenario run.

**The subject.** `manifest.subject` is `{"kind": "test-run", "title", "scenario": {"id", "sha256"}, "system_under_test": {"repository", "commit", "image_digest" | null}, "environment": {"name", "sha256"}, "runner": {"id", "machine_id"}, "started_at", "finished_at"}`. `environment` has the scenario's shape: its descriptor is an artefact of the run, named by hash. `started_at` is not after `finished_at`, and `taken_at` is `finished_at`, exactly. `runner.machine_id` is the enrolment's public id, never a hardware serial or a hostname: the record is made to be public.

**The auditor's part: exactly three claims**, written by the party that ran it (the runner, a CI job, `deploy/helm/test.sh`), in the agent-work row shape (one `evidence` list):
- `C-001`, kind `process`: we ran these inputs. Its evidence names the scenario file, the system-under-test descriptor and the environment descriptor, each by hash.
- `C-002`, kind `figure`: we got these outputs. Its evidence names the result (the pass list or the run result, with every named check and its verdict) and the logs by correlation id, each by hash, and states the duration.
- `C-003`, kind `other`: what this tells you, one sentence the run supports and nothing more, its scope and its caveats inside it, and it names the commit of the system under test ("at commit 917612a the plane refused a batch with a negative sequence", never "the plane is secure"). "Names the commit" means the text contains a prefix of `subject.system_under_test.commit` at least seven characters long, compared case-insensitively; a bare seven-hex string that is not that prefix does not count. The writer refuses a C-003 that fails this before any file is written, and the checker holds the rule (K11). Its evidence is the result.
A fourth evidence shape is added for this profile: `{"artefact": "<name>", "sha256": "<hex>"}`, an artefact of the run by hash. Its source entry carries the same name (as `path` or `name`).

**Where the evidence lives: the evidence locator.** `manifest.evidence_locator` is `{"held_by": "folder" | "fleet" | "blob" | "subject-org", "where": "<url or path>" | null, "expires_at": "<time>" | null, "note"}`. With `folder` every artefact is in `sources/artefacts/`, and one artefact source without its copy there fails K1. Otherwise the artefact's source entry is `hash-only`, and the locator says who holds the bytes and until when: the Fleet store (hashes, or the bytes under F19), a blob store beside the plane, or the subject's own organisation (a customer pipeline, its own expiry). The record never depends on the holder to stay intact: its hashes and signatures check offline; only the reading of the bytes needs the holder.

**Signing.** The auditor's part is signed as in section 12, by an enrolled key. A CI job or `deploy/helm/test.sh` has no machine of its own, so the plane's own suites sign with a key enrolled for the CI identity, with the repository's owner as the human who stands behind it. Until that key exists, the CLI writes the record unsigned (`--allow-unsigned`, said in the manifest) and K13 fails by design: an unsigned run record is a claim nobody stands behind, and the checker says so.

**The witness.** A different key from the runner's, always (as in section 12). Two methods; in this profile the witness header's `method` is exactly `read` or `re-run`, and anything else fails K11:
- `read`: the witness fetches the artefacts from the locator, checks each against its hash, reads the named checks in the result, and decides each claim. Under `read`, C-001 rests on artefacts the runner itself wrote, so a read witness evidences that the record is consistent with itself; only `re-run` tests the inputs independently.
- `re-run`: the witness runs the same scenario against the same pinned system under test and compares the named checks and their verdicts with C-002. It never compares output bytes: model outputs are distributions, and a re-run of an agent scenario is not byte-identical. The header names the re-run's own result, `"rerun_result": {"artefact", "sha256"}`, without which the comparison is a bare claim (K11). A disagreement on a named check is CONTRADICTED on C-002, with the pointer to the result in `on.counter`. With `image_digest: null` the system under test is a commit, not a binary: the witness builds it again, and its header's `note` says so as a warning (no checker rule).
Under either method, an artefact FETCHED from the locator whose bytes do not hash to the recorded value contradicts the claim whose evidence names it, with that pointer in `on.counter`, whatever else it shows. A re-run's own artefacts are fresh bytes: they are never hashed against the record, only compared by named check and verdict.

**What the checker does differently for this profile** (on top of section 12's rules, which apply to the evidence list):
- K5/K6: an `artefact` pointer is verified when a source with that sha256 has its copy in the folder AND that source carries the pointer's name (its `path` or `name`); a hash that resolves under another name FAILs that pointer. Held elsewhere it is untestable (WARN), as a hash-only source is. A hash that no source lists FAILs that pointer whoever holds the bytes: the sources are the record's inventory, and the auditor's evidence is inside it or the record is inconsistent.
- K11: the auditor's part has exactly the three rows `C-001`, `C-002`, `C-003`, with those kinds, and C-003's text contains a prefix of `subject.system_under_test.commit` of seven characters or more (case-insensitive). Each witness header's `method` is `read` or `re-run`, and a `re-run` header carries `rerun_result` with an artefact name and a sha256. A `rerun_result` whose sha256 no source in the folder holds is a comparison nobody can check: WARN.
- K1: the subject's fields above (`scenario` and `environment` each with `name`/`id` and `sha256`) and `evidence_locator` are present; `held_by` is in the vocabulary, else FAIL; with another holder than the folder, `where` names it (a url or path), else FAIL, the rule the writer already holds; with `held_by: folder`, every artefact source has its copy in `sources/artefacts/`, else FAIL; `started_at` is not after `finished_at` and `taken_at` equals `finished_at`, else FAIL; `expires_at`, when set, is a time.
- K14: an anchor is optional, as everywhere; "on chain" in the direction is met by an anchor over the signatures file (armarium when it ships, Argus's chains meanwhile, D75), and until one exists the check says so (WARN).

**What a run record is not.** It proves that this run happened as described, on that day, at that commit. It does not prove the product correct, and the scope sentence is the claim's own limit.

**Writers** (built with this section): the kit writes one per scenario run (`onedroid runner` returns it as `run_result.run_record`, F18), and a CLI writes one for any suite (the plane's `go test` and `deploy/helm/test.sh`). The code is one Go package beside the kit's `record` package, used by the kit's bundle (section 12), the runner and the plane, so the three subjects keep one shape.
